How it works

No spreadsheets, no guessing from one lucky sale. Every morning, a robot does the boring part.

Scan eBay

We search eBay US for every toy and card in each Happy Meal series and collect every Buy It Now listing.

Throw out the noise

Lots, full sets, graded slabs, fakes and listings for other years are filtered out, leaving single, ungraded items.

One fair price

We add each listing's shipping, take the median of what's left (so one silly $500 listing can't move the number), and follow listings day to day to spot what actually sells.

What the numbers mean

Asking priceThe median of today's matching listings, including shipping to the US: half ask more, half ask less.
Lowest priceThe cheapest listing today, shipping included: roughly what you'd pay to buy one right now.
Buyers payWhat it actually sold for, shipping included, once Toy Ticker has spotted 5 or more sales. Usually below the asking price.
ListingsExisting listings: everything still for sale on eBay today.
Sells out inHow long today's listings would last at the recent pace of sales. Short means buyers snap them up; long means plenty to choose from.
Complete setWhat a whole set listed on eBay typically costs, shipping included, next to buying every item separately.
ReturnsHow much a price moved over 1 day, 1 week, 1 month and so on, shown once there's enough history.
Estimated valueWhat your haul in My Happy Meals is worth: what each item sells for once it has 5+ sales, its asking price otherwise.
ConfidenceHow much to trust a price. High means lots of listings that mostly agree; Low means only a few, so one listing can move it.

Heads up: sellers usually ask more than buyers end up paying. Before you price something, check Buyers pay in the Items list or the green Sold line on the item's chart.

Questions

Where do the prices come from?

Once a day, early morning US time, Toy Ticker collects every matching eBay US Buy It Now listing. Each counts as item price plus its cheapest shipping to the US (a central ZIP code when shipping varies by distance, or the item's typical shipping when eBay gives none). The headline prices are the median of those asking prices. Toy Ticker also follows the listings day to day: when one sells, it records what it sold for, shipping included. That's the green Sold line on charts and Buyers pay in the Items list. eBay doesn't share sold data with most apps, so this is an estimate, and a sale is counted the day it's spotted.

How do "Buyers pay" and "Sells out in" work?

A listing counts as sold when eBay shows it sold out with sales, or its sold count goes up. Buyers pay is the typical price those sales went for, shown once an item has 5 or more. Time to sell is the typical number of days from listing to sale. Sells out in is today's listings divided by recent sales per day: how long they'd last if nobody listed more, shown as 60+ beyond 60 days. Items with under 3 sales or 5 listings aren't rated, nor are items whose search only follows a small share of their listings, because the numbers would mislead.

Why is a price or photo missing?

If nobody has listed a single, ungraded one yet, there's nothing fair to measure. Most photos are official: toy photos from McDonald's Happy Meal site (happymeal.com) and Pokémon card images from pokemon.com. The rest come from eBay listings and link to them. Each photo is credited on the item's page.

Is my collection private?

Yes. What you mark in My Happy Meals is saved only in your browser. There are no accounts and no tracking, and nothing about your collection is sent anywhere. Clearing your browser data clears it.

Does Toy Ticker make money? Is this advice?

Links to eBay go through the eBay Partner Network, so if you buy after clicking one, eBay may pay Toy Ticker a small commission. It costs you nothing extra and never changes which listings or prices are shown. Toy Ticker is free and ad-free; if it helped you, you can leave a tip on Ko-fi ☕. It isn't investment advice: Happy Meal toys are made by the million and prices can swing on a single listing. Collect what you love; use Toy Ticker to avoid over- or under-paying.
